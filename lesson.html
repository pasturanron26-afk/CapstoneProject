Shared Organelles

Nucleus

Both Cells

The nucleus is the most prominent, double-membrane-bound genetic organelle inside eukaryotic cells. It acts as the cell's control center, storing linear DNA (genetic blueprint) and directing metabolism, growth, protein synthesis, and reproduction.

Function

Controls gene expression and directs all cell activity. It transcribes genetic DNA sequences into messenger RNA (mRNA) transcripts, which exit through nuclear pores into the cytoplasm to guide ribosomal protein synthesis.

Mitochondria

Both Cells

Mitochondria are double-membrane-bound, rod- or oval-shaped cytoplasmic organelles present in eukaryotic cells. They are the "powerhouses" of the cell, responsible for making adenosine triphosphate (ATP), the primary short-term energy currency of the cell.

Function

Produces ATP energy through cellular respiration. The fluid-filled matrix hosts the Krebs cycle to break down nutrients, while the folded inner cristae host the Electron Transport Chain to generate large amounts of ATP via oxidative phosphorylation.

Ribosomes

Both Cells

Ribosomes are small, non-membrane-bound cellular structures made of RNA and proteins. They are complex macromolecular structures that serve as the sites of translation, responsible for protein synthesis.

Function

Synthesizes polypeptide chains (proteins). They receive trans-located mRNA strands from the nucleus and translate the triplet codon sequence into a specific sequence of amino acids, utilizing tRNA to bring and hook amino acids into a polypeptide chain.

Golgi Apparatus

Both Cells

The Golgi apparatus is an endomembrane system organelle consisting of compact stacks of flattened membranes. It modifies, sorts, packages, tags, and distributes proteins and lipids synthesized in the ER to their proper destinations inside or outside the cell.

Function

Processes and modifies proteins by adding sugar chains to make glycoproteins. Transport vesicles from the ER fuse with the cis face, cargo travels through cisternae where enzymes modify it, then it's sorted, tagged, packed into secretory vesicles, and shipped from the trans face.

Endoplasmic Reticulum

Both Cells

The ER is an extensive, folded network of interconnected membranous sacs and tubules extending from the outer nuclear envelope. It provides a supporting framework and serves as the primary site for the synthesis, folding, modification, and transport of lipids, proteins, and carbohydrates.

Function

Rough ER uses attached ribosomes to synthesize proteins directly into its lumen, where they are folded, combined with sugars, packed into vesicles, and sent to the Golgi. Smooth ER uses bound membrane enzymes to synthesize lipids/steroids and metabolize carbohydrates and toxins.

Cell Membrane

Both Cells

The cell membrane is a thin, selectively permeable biological barrier that encloses the cytoplasm. It separates the internal contents of the cell from its external environment and strictly regulates the passage of materials entering and exiting the cell.

Function

Regulates and transports nutrients, mineral ions, and water inside while expelling cellular waste. The hydrophobic lipid bilayer allows small and non-polar molecules to pass through passively, while embedded trans-membrane proteins act as transport channels or pumps.

Vacuole

Both Cells

A vacuole is a membrane-bound, fluid-filled storage sac located in the cytoplasm of eukaryotic cells. Its primary function is to store water, nutrients, carbohydrates, and enzymes, while isolating toxic wastes and poorly folded proteins to protect the rest of the cell.

Animal vs. Plant Cells: While both cell types contain vacuoles, they differ significantly in size and function. Animal cells contain multiple small, temporary vacuoles used for short-term storage, transport, and waste removal (such as in endocytosis). In contrast, mature plant cells feature a single, massive central vacuole that can occupy up to 90% of the cell's total volume.

Function

The surrounding membrane (tonoplast) utilizes active transport to strictly control the passage of materials. In plant cells, when the central vacuole is filled with water, it exerts vital turgor pressure against the rigid cell wall, stretching the cell and providing crucial structural support without the need to synthesize new cytoplasm. In animal cells, vacuoles are highly dynamic, rapidly changing shape to sequester toxic substances or aid in cellular digestion.

Cytoskeleton

Both Cells

The cytoskeleton is an intricate, non-membrane-bound network of structural protein fibers that spans the entire cytoplasm from the cell membrane to the nucleus. Its primary function is to maintain cell shape, structurally reinforce the cell, anchor organelles, and coordinate cell movement and internal transport.

Overall Function

It maintains the cell's physical shape, structurally reinforces the cell against external pressure, anchors organelles in specific positions, and coordinates internal transport and cellular movement (such as muscle contraction or cell crawling).

The 3 Components of the Cytoskeleton

1. Microtubules

What it is: The thickest of the cytoskeletal fibers, composed of hollow cylinders made of the protein tubulin.

Function: They resist compression and serve as the cell's internal "transit tracks." Motor proteins (like kinesin and dynein) walk along them to transport vesicles and organelles. They also form the mitotic spindle that pulls chromosomes apart during cell division, and make up the core structure of cilia and flagella.

microtubules by SourLobster5747 on Sketchfab

2. Intermediate Filaments

What it is: Mid-sized, rope-like fibers made of various fibrous proteins (such as keratin in animal cells). They are the most permanent and stable of the three filament types.

Function: They provide tensile strength and elasticity, helping the cell withstand mechanical stress and stretching. They also act as a structural scaffold to anchor organelles (like the nucleus) firmly in place.

Intermediate Filament by SourLobster5747 on Sketchfab

3. Microfilaments (Actin Filaments)

What it is: The thinnest cytoskeletal fibers, composed of two intertwined strands of the globular protein actin.

Function: They resist tension and are crucial for cell motility (crawling). In muscle cells, they pair with myosin to drive rapid contractions. They also facilitate cytoplasmic streaming and form the "cleavage furrow" that pinches a cell in two during division.

Animal Cell Only

Lysosome

Animal Only

The lysosome is a small, spherical, single-membrane-bound vesicle found primarily in animal cells that contains an array of highly acidic digestive enzymes. It serves as the cellular waste disposal and recycling facility.

Function

Digests old or damaged cellular components, breaks down food particles, and destroys foreign pathogens. It maintains an internal acidic pH of 5 using a specialized membrane-bound proton pump that activates hydrolytic enzymes to safely break down macromolecules.

lysosome by SourLobster5747 on Sketchfab

Centriole

Animal Only

A centriole is a small, hollow, cylindrical, non-membrane-bound structure that resides inside the centrosome of animal cells. It functions as the principal microtubule-organizing center (MTOC), coordinating the cellular microtubule network.

Function

Organizes the mitotic spindle during cell division and builds locomotory structures like cilia and flagella. It anchors existing microtubules and uses molecular factors in its surrounding matrix to synthesize and grow new tubules outward.

Plant Cell Only

Cell Wall

Plant Only

The cell wall is the outermost, rigid protective layer located outside the cell membrane in plants, algae, fungi, and some bacteria. It provides mechanical strength, support, rigidity, and protection, and maintains the cell's distinct shape and overall integrity.

Function

Provides mechanical strength and maintains cell shape. Microtubules guide the assembly of cellulose fibers, which are laid down by enzymes to form the primary wall. When cell growth is complete, some plants build a secondary wall containing lignin to offer permanent stiff mechanical support.

cell-wall by SourLobster5747 on Sketchfab

Chloroplast

Plant Only

The chloroplast is a double-membrane-bound, pigment-containing plastid unique to photosynthetic plants and algae. It is the site of photosynthesis, capturing solar energy to convert water and carbon dioxide into organic sugars (like glucose) and oxygen.

Function

Performs photosynthesis. During light-dependent reactions, chlorophyll in the thylakoid membranes absorbs sunlight to create molecular energy. During light-independent reactions, this energy is used in the stroma to synthesize glucose from carbon dioxide and water.
